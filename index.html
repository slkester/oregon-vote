Your Oregon ballot is here. Vote all the way down.

Every registered voter in Oregon gets a ballot in the mail. Here's how to get yours back on time, what you'll find on it, and why the races at the bottom matter as much as the ones at the top.

Key dates

Oregon votes by mail, so the deadline that matters is when your ballot gets back, not when you show up somewhere.

Nov. 3

Return Your Ballot

Where's my drop box?

There are three ways to get your ballot back. Whichever one you pick, sign the return envelope first.

01

Drop box

The surest way to get your ballot in on time. Official drop boxes are open 24/7 from the time ballots are mailed until 8 p.m. on Election Day.

Find the drop box nearest you

02

Mail

Postage is prepaid, so no stamp needed. Mail it by Oct. 27 to be safe. Closer to Election Day, get a free manual postmark at a post office counter instead of using a mailbox.

03

County elections office

Drop your ballot off in person at your county elections office during the voting period. Check your county's hours before you go.

You can use any official drop box in Oregon, even one outside your county. Your ballot gets routed home to be counted.

Questions

Oregon voting FAQ

Quick answers for voting in Oregon.

Is a sample ballot the same as my official ballot?

No. A sample ballot is a preview of the races and measures that will likely appear on your ballot, so you can research and decide ahead of time. In Oregon, your official ballot comes in the mail from your county elections office, and that official ballot is the only one you can cast.

Why does my address matter for what's on my ballot?

Your ballot depends on exactly where you live. Two neighbors a few streets apart can be in different congressional, legislative, school, or city districts, so your address is what determines the races that are specific to you.

What kinds of races will be on my ballot?

Depending on the year and where you live, you may see federal races like U.S. Senate and House, statewide offices like governor and attorney general, state legislative seats, and local offices like county commissioner, sheriff, city council, and school board. Many ballots also include judges and ballot measures. This November, every Oregon ballot includes governor, labor commissioner, and U.S. Senate, plus your U.S. House seat.

What is a ballot measure?

A ballot measure is a question that voters decide directly, such as a proposed law, a constitutional amendment, a tax or bond, or a change to local rules. Depending on your state, these are called initiatives, levies, referendums, propositions, or amendments. There are no statewide measures on Oregon's ballot this November, but many communities have local ones.

Why should I vote in every race on my ballot?

School boards, county offices, judges, and local seats are often decided by a few hundred votes or less, and extremist candidates count on voters stopping after the top of the ticket. These are the offices that decide what your kids learn, how your elections are run, and which rights get protected in your own community. You don’t have to vote in every race for your ballot to count, but voting all the way down is how we keep extremists out of office.

Can I use my sample ballot or notes when I vote?

Yes. Oregon votes by mail, so you fill out your ballot at home, at your own pace. Keep your sample ballot, the Voters’ Pamphlet, or your notes nearby while you fill it out.

Where is my polling place?

Oregon doesn’t use neighborhood polling places. You vote at home and return your ballot by mail, at an official drop box, or at your county elections office. If you need help voting or an accessible way to vote, contact your county elections office, and confirm drop site locations and hours with them before Election Day.

What is on my ballot?

What’s on your ballot depends on where you live. Your ballot may include federal, state, and local races as well as judges, school boards, and ballot measures. In Oregon, the state Voters’ Pamphlet, plus your county’s pamphlet if it publishes one, covers the candidates and measures, and nonprofit lookups like Vote411 can show you a sample ballot for your address.

Why should I make a plan before I fill out my ballot?

Sitting down with a long ballot is a lot of pressure, and it’s hard to remember every candidate and measure you looked into. Doing your research ahead of time, at your own pace, means you get to your ballot ready to make informed choices in every race.

Where's my drop box?

Oregon has official ballot drop boxes in every county, often at libraries, city halls, and county offices. Use the Secretary of State's drop box locator at OregonVotes.gov to find the one closest to you. Drop boxes are open around the clock until 8 p.m. on Election Day.

Do I need a stamp to mail my ballot?

No. Return postage on Oregon ballots is prepaid. Just sign your envelope, seal it, and drop it in the mail. If it's after Oct. 27, use a drop box or get a manual postmark at a post office counter so your ballot is postmarked by Election Day.

Can I drop off my ballot in a different county?

Yes. You can use any official drop box in Oregon. The county that collects it will send it to your home county to be counted.

Can I drop off a ballot for someone in my household?

Yes. You can return a ballot for someone else, but it has to be delivered to an official drop box or elections office within two days of receiving it, or by 8 p.m. on Election Day, whichever comes first.

My ballot hasn't arrived. What do I do?

Contact your county elections office as soon as possible. They can check your registration and send you a replacement ballot if you need one.

What if there's a problem with my signature?

Your signature is how your county verifies your ballot, so don't skip it. If there's a problem with your signature, your county elections office will contact you with how to fix it. Keep an eye on your mail, and track your ballot at OregonVotes.gov/MyVote.
